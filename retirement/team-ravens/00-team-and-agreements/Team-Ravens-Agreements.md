### Team Principles

- **Collaboration:**  Work toward shared outcomes.
- **Transparency:**  Make progress, risks, and decisions visible.

#### How we engage

- **Respect:**  Assume positive intent; challenge ideas, not people.
- **Psychological Safety:**  Welcome questions and different perspectives.

#### How we improve

- **Ownership:**  Clarify responsibilities and ask for help early.
- **Continuous Improvement:**  Inspect and adapt our practices.

### Working Agreements

#### Communication

- Clarify requirements, outcomes, and assumptions.
- Share decisions, changes, and availability visibly.
- Capture information needed by absent team members.

#### Work visibility

- Keep Azure DevOps (ADO), formerly known as Team Foundation Server (TFS), statuses, tasks, estimates, and blockers current.
  - Update the task or user story immediately after work is completed.
- Show dependencies, scope changes, and deferred work.
- Link supporting documents and related items.
  - Keep user stories updated with discussion outcomes and questions and answers.

#### Roles and handoffs

- Clarify who develops, reviews, tests, validates, promotes, and confirms.
- Add promotion tasks with module details before release.
  - **Roll to Production conventions**
  - Use clear, descriptive user story names.
  - Follow the user story naming convention and include a date.
  - A user story with a Roll to Production task moves to Done when Development and Test are complete.
- Confirm handoffs across Product, BA, Dev, QA, SMEs, and deployment owners.

#### Flow and meetings

- Favor finishing and validating work over starting more.
- Keep the Daily Scrum focused on goals, progress, blockers, and help needed.
  - Align meeting attendance with the topic.
- Move technical detail to focused follow-ups; respect focus time.

#### Quality and releases

- Do not promote before required testing and validation.
- Create the required TrakIt record, SharePoint page, and meeting invitation for a Roll to Production.
- Test affected offerings, shared modules, dependencies, and end-to-end flow.
- Prepare release scope, owners, modules, and SharePoint details before deployment.
  - Use a brief planning session to set expectations for urgent Production releases.
- Keep unrelated changes outside urgent release paths.
- Track Production changes separately; maintain documentation in SharePoint.
- Production deployments may occur whenever needed, as soon and as often as appropriate, provided required testing, validation, documentation, ownership, and release controls are completed.

### Definition of Ready

#### Required

- Value, outcome, scope, and acceptance criteria are understood.
- Testing and validation expectations are clear.
- Priority, estimate, owner, and tasks are identified.
- Fits within one sprint or has a splitting plan.
  - A rollover may occur but should be avoided when plan-splitting is not valid. [Rollover should be exceptional and used only when work cannot be meaningfully split without losing value or increasing delivery risk.]
- No open issue prevents commitment.

#### When applicable

- Dependencies and affected offerings are identified.
- Cross-team ownership and QA or SME capacity are confirmed.
- Release impact is understood.

### Definition of Done

#### Required

- Acceptance criteria are met.
- Development, developer testing, and required review are complete.
  - Consider an AI-powered code-review tool, as used by Team White, to improve code-error detection.
- QA or SME validation is complete.
- No defect or subtask prevents the intended outcome.
- ADO records completion and supporting evidence for the intended outcome.
- Add the task for promoting the user story to Production to the “Prod Deployment Task” in the sprint.
