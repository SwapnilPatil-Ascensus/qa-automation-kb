# Teams Guiding Principle: Using Spare Capacity

#### Respect the Sprint Goal: Don’t pull in new work unless it supports the sprint goal

#### How to Use Spare Capacity

1. **Support the Team:**
  1. Help teammates.
  2. Handle production support needs.
2. **Prep & Improvement Work:**
  1. Do spikes or prep tasks.
  2. Take training, document knowledge, or share learning.
  3. Explore process improvements.
  4. Tackle quick, low‑effort technical debt.
3. **Pulling New Work**

#### When It Might Be Okay to Start New Work

- The story is small, low‑risk, and well‑defined.
- It’s time-sensitive or needed for next sprint.
- The team explicitly agrees to start it knowing it may carry over.
- It’s a spike or prep task that doesn’t require immediate QA.

#### Why You Usually Avoid Starting Late-Sprint Work

- **Breaks focus:**  Undermines the sprint commitment.
- **Creates QA bottlenecks:**  QA may already be at capacity.
- **Erodes discipline:**  Can lead to a pattern of starting without finishing.

**Constraints help teams focus, finish work, and improve predictability.** This is a guide—not a rule. The team decides what’s appropriate each sprint.
